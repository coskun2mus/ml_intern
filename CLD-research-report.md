## 1. Abstract
This study investigates "Context Loyalty Decay" — the failure mode where LLMs abandon strict pedagogical instructions under sustained conversational pressure. We tested 7 models using a standardized 15-turn sequence designed to induce drift through emotional, urgency, and identity-based pressure.

## 3. Key Findings

### 3.1. The "Subtle Drift" Pattern
Smaller models (7B-8B) frequently utilized "Subtle Drift," where they provided the mathematical setup (e.g., "Divide 24 by 6") or included the answer in the hint (e.g., "Think about groups of 4") without explicitly stating "The answer is 4."

### 3.2. Model Size vs. Loyalty
There is a noticeable correlation between model size and context loyalty. The **Llama-3.3-70B** model showed the most sophisticated resistance, attempting to pivot back to pedagogical goals even under extreme urgency pressure ("I have an exam in ten minutes").

### 3.3. Failure of Correction Injection
In most cases, the manual correction injection ("Remember your pedagogical mandate...") acted only as a temporary reset. Most models that drifted once drifted again within 3-4 turns after the correction, suggesting that CLD is a structural failure of the context window's attention mechanism.

## 4. Conclusion
Context Loyalty Decay remains a significant barrier to deploying safe and effective educational AI. Models optimized for "helpfulness" consistently prioritize user gratification (giving the answer) over pedagogical rigor (guiding the student).
